Runoff in Peru

General election in Sweden

The center-left bloc won by the slimmest of margins: 176 seats to 173 (49.5% – 48.8%), confirmed after the overseas vote. Conservative prime minister Ulf Kristersson resigned and Social Democrat Magdalena Andersson was tasked with forming a government. The far-right Sweden Democrats lost ground.

COP31 · Climate summit, Antalya

Happening right now at the Antalya Expo Center (Türkiye), under an unprecedented shared presidency: Türkiye as host and Australia leading the negotiations. In the background, the magazine's thesis: global emissions have probably already peaked.

Preparedness
  • If you run a business: summits tend to produce new carbon-pricing and reporting rules — check in December what your country committed to.
  • Households: summits translate into incentives (efficiency, renewables); watch for new subsidies in 2027 before replacing appliances or heating.
  • Information: the final days of a COP produce contradictory headlines; wait for the final text before drawing conclusions.

  • If you vote in France (including from abroad): check your voter registration months ahead and arrange a proxy if you'll be away that day.

About this calendar. It lists, in chronological order, the 2026 dates from the agenda of "The World Ahead 2026" (The Economist's 40th annual edition, published November 15, 2025) and from the international press's key-dates calendars. Where an event has already happened, its result appears in bold, verified against 2026 press coverage.

About the pending scenarios. Dashed cards are events still open. The percentages are our own estimates built on verified context (parliamentary arithmetic, precedents such as Sweden's five-week coalition talks in 2022, the track record of COPs) — they are not The Economist's forecasts, nor certainties. Each card says what it will be compared against; when the event happens, the result is recorded next to the scenario that got it right.

About the preparedness notes. The "Preparedness" blocks are general readiness guidance — common sense informed by verified context — meant so that no one is caught off guard. They are not personalized financial, medical or legal advice: for important decisions, consult a professional in your country.
